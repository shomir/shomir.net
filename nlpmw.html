Read This First

I wrote a paper about research communication that was accepted by EMNLP 2026. The advice in it is broadly applicable to speaking with news media about artificial intelligence (AI) research, even though the title refers to natural language processing (NLP). It's an unusual NLP conference paper that contains no experiments, no system building, no algorithms, and no data. It's readable by a general audience, including AI or NLP researchers who are just getting started and people outside research.

I'm sharing some behind-the scenes notes about this paper, including commentary on its contents and the timeline that led to its acceptance. (Prior to EMNLP 2026, it was rejected by IJCNLP-AACL 2025 and ACL 2026.) Also, although it's a paper with a sincere message, the commentary reveals some metatextual humor. I think research writing can have depth and nuance, and under certain circumstances, it can be written for appreciation on multiple levels.
